About

From cell culture rooms in Monterrey to dynamical models in Houston. The question stayed the same: what does a cell do with the nutrients it has?

I am a PhD candidate in Biomedical Sciences at Texas A&M University School of Medicine, working with Jason T. George on mathematical and statistical models of complex biological systems. My work sits at the junction of cancer metabolism, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, and machine learning. I build mechanistic models (ODE and constraint-based) and learned models (linear-response, variational), and I spend as much effort on the nulls and baselines as on the models themselves.

Before computation I spent eight years at the bench at Tecnológico de Monterrey and two start-ups: CAR-T manufacturing, GMP mesenchymal stem cell production, primary immune cells, CRISPR-Cas9 in CHO cells, organ-on-chip models and potency assays. That background is why I distrust a result that has not been tried against the simplest explanation first.

Outside the lab I have taught high-school students the statistics behind random walks, chaired the organizing committee of Biolaunch, a bio-entrepreneurship community in Mexico and Latin America, and mentored students since my undergraduate years.

Redundancy and load restrict predicted metabolic rerouting to the respiratory chain

Where does metabolic flux go when a bioenergetic gene is knocked down? Constraint-based flux solutions on Human-GEM 2.1.1, with expression mapped through GPRs and growth calibrated per line, computed across six human lines from four CRISPRi screens, then tested for rerouting against two nulls at once, a control null and a permutation null, both at q below 0.05, with every call required to be identifiable under flux variability analysis and not merely tracking growth. Of the candidate rerouting events that reach the null stage, 82 respiratory and 41 non-respiratory, 31 respiratory survive and none of the non-respiratory ones do. The surviving calls reduce to 19 unique predicted rerouting events at the primary calibration, and 17 and 16 when the growth fraction is swept to 0.3 and 0.7, so the result is not an artefact of where the calibration sits. Redundancy and load, not topology, are what close the alternative routes. A nine-subunit knockdown panel on complex III in K562 and a per-line comparison against DepMap essentiality test the same claim from the other direction. The control growth fraction was fixed twelve days before the first census was computed, and null-stage attrition is reported by class, so a reader can see the non-respiratory classes go to zero rather than being filtered out quietly. These are model-derived flux solutions, not measured fluxes.

Manuscript in preparationGeorge Lab

Manufacturing, not patient biology, in CAR-T infusion products

Whether a CAR-T product's transcriptomic state predicts whether the patient responds. Across three cohorts the axis that reproduces between products is the manufacturing batch, not the patient, and the product-type axis separates at p = 0.0001 even within a single batch. No score of product state transfers. An L2 logistic model on pseudobulk principal components reaches 0.684 in inner cross-validation and 0.524 on a held-out cohort; a gated-attention multiple-instance model trained on the same 54 products reaches 0.730 and 0.483. Split-half reproducibility of the product-state axis runs 0.43 to 0.90 depending on cohort size, which is the ceiling any such score has to clear and explains why none of them do. CD8 fraction is the one quantity that behaves consistently across all three.

JaviCell, Arc Virtual Cell Challenge 2026

Zero-shot prediction of single-cell CRISPRi responses in a cell type never seen in training. Per-knockdown effects are borrowed from five public screens, combined by inverse-variance consensus with adaptive shrinkage, partially pooled across knockdowns, then written onto the target context's own control cells and emitted as 400 cells per knockdown. Run under a frozen rule book with gates set in advance and a signed deviation log, and the log is the interesting part. The selected recipe failed to build in one of the three final contexts after 200 of 300 targets, deterministically, and the automatic fallback was refused by a checker whose rule predated one of the recipe's own parameters. The final entry therefore ships the fallback recipe across all three contexts with one knockdown, E/SLC30A1, byte-copied from a pinned earlier build, under a deviation signed before the build rather than after it. Final entry submitted 25 October 2026 and scored on the test partition.

LRJ, a linear-response model of knockdown effects

A dynamical model with learned low-rank regulatory feedback, stable by construction (bounded gains, spectral-norm constraint). Trained on 15,430 pseudobulk knockdown profiles across 10 contexts and evaluated leave-one-cell-line-out. On held-out lines it beat a 3.9M-parameter conditional VAE (knockdown-specific Spearman 0.06 to 0.12 vs about 0, 95% CIs exclude zero) and passed the shuffled-label validity check the VAE failed.

Python, PyTorch

Benchmarks of AlphaFold-era tools

Four tests of derived structural features against simple baselines. Predicted enzyme kcats (DLKcat re-implemented, 19,733 Human-GEM reaction-enzyme pairs) made enzyme-constrained FBA better at K562 essentiality than plain FBA (+0.05 AUC, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.09). AlphaFold-Multimer interaction confidence against Perturb-seq (594,750 pairs), AlphaMissense somatic selection (147,346 TCGA mutations) and AlphaFold pocket druggability (543 proteins) were all negative under pre-set bars while positive controls passed.

boltzbench, does Boltz-2 know its regulators?

A CPU benchmark of whether Boltz-2 ranks a human metabolic enzyme's known allosteric regulator above six decoys. v1 came out positive but uninformative, since most of its complexes are probably in the training data. v2 rebuilt the panel around that problem: 32 enzyme-metabolite pairs with no PDB co-structure at any deposition date and no deposited binding measurement, analysis plan hashed before pair selection. It passed, 0.766 against a 0.599 bar, p below 1e-5. Then two cheap post-hoc checks took it apart. Ranking the same seven molecules by molecular weight alone, with no protein and no model, scores 0.854. On the 24 pairs weight already solves, Boltz-2 scores 0.826; on the 8 it does not, 0.583, indistinguishable from chance. A literature audit of the positive class then found 14 of the 32 regulations do not hold as labelled: four invalid for the human protein or not regulations at all, three keyed to the wrong subunit, seven resting on non-human evidence or a different mechanism. Both post-hoc findings survive the audit. The lesson is about benchmark construction rather than about Boltz-2: a fixed analysis plan disciplined the statistic, the null and the selection rule, and protected neither the decoy panel nor the labels.
